## 2. IBM and Siemens Form Strategic Alliance to Commercialize 3D "Nanostack" Transistor Architecture Beyond 7-Angstrom

### Key Takeaways
On October 14, 2026, **IBM** and industrial automation leader **Siemens** entered into a major strategic research and software development alliance aimed at scaling IBM’s proprietary **3D "nanostack" transistor architecture** toward high-volume commercial production. First unveiled conceptually by IBM Research earlier in 2026, nanostack represents the semiconductor industry’s first monolithic 3D nanosheet architecture, shifting standard device scaling from planar horizontal dimensions into vertically stacked, staggered transistor arrays. At its baseline **7-angstrom (0.7-nanometer) design node**, the nanostack architecture succeeds in packing nearly **100 billion transistors onto a die no larger than a human fingernail**, establishing a transformative technological pathway for next-generation AI accelerators and quantum-classical compute engines.

The physical engineering breakthrough of nanostack lies in its modular vertical stacking. Unlike traditional horizontal gate-all-around (GAA) nanosheets or complementary FET (CFET) architectures, nanostack staggers n-type and p-type field-effect transistors into discrete vertical layers, enabling disparate material compositions to be integrated within the same vertical column. This decoupled architecture allows engineers to optimize electron mobility in the lower channel using silicon-germanium while tuning upper conduction layers for thermal dissipation and minimal gate leakage. However, fabricating 3D nanostack structures at sub-1nm scales introduces unprecedented lithographic and material stress challenges, including localized parasitic capacitance, severe mechanical strain between atomic monolayers, and thermal trapping within middle transistor layers.

Under the partnership, Siemens is deploying its flagship **Electronic Design Automation (EDA)** software suite, multiphysics simulation frameworks, and computational lithography toolsets to build comprehensive digital twins of the nanostack fabrication process. By pairing IBM’s physical device physics and Albany NanoTech test-fab data with Siemens’ advanced simulation algorithms, the joint engineering teams will virtually model electrostatic behavior, predict fabrication defects, and optimize chemical mechanical polishing (CMP) tolerances before silicon wafers enter physical lithography tracks. The collaboration establishes an integrated design-technology co-optimization (DTCO) workflow designed to deliver certified process design kits (PDKs) to commercial semiconductor foundries and design houses targeting nodes below 5 angstroms.

### Why It Matters
As silicon lithography reaches the atomic precipice below 1 nanometer, classical geometric scaling (Dennard scaling) is physically exhausted, threatening to stall the hardware compute density gains that underpin exponential AI progress. IBM and Siemens’ collaboration on 3D nanostack proves that vertical dimensional scaling is the definitive solution to the "silicon wall," preserving Moore’s Law by moving upward rather than outward. By providing commercial EDA tooling and virtual modeling for 3D nanosheet stacking, Siemens and IBM are de-risking one of the most complex manufacturing transitions in microelectronics history. This opens the door for merchant foundries and fabless chipmakers to design ultra-dense AI processors that deliver up to a 40% performance-per-watt advantage without exceeding data center thermal and physical footprint limits.
